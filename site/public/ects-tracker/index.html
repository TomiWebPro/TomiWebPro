ECTS Tracker

Problem & Student Workflow

In European universities operating under the European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System (ECTS), one ECTS credit officially represents 25 to 30 hours of academic workload. For an undergraduate taking 30 ECTS per semester at TUM, this translates to approximately 900 hours across lectures, exercises, lab practicals, and independent exam preparation.

ECTS Tracker was built in Budapest in November 2026 to pace remote TUM study hours during German student visa processing delays. Most student calendar tools fail to connect abstract credit requirements to daily time investment. ECTS Tracker bridges this gap: students input their enrolled modules, exam dates, and lecture times, and the app computes rolling daily pacing targets to prevent last-minute cramming. It has been shared with TUM classmates facing the same remote-study constraints.

Local-First Architecture

ECTS Tracker is built with strict privacy and local-first principles:

  • Reactive State with Riverpod: State providers reactively propagate study session logs, course progress metrics, and target adjustments across desktop and mobile screens.
  • Type-Safe Persistence with Drift: Built on SQLite using Drift (formerly Moor), offering reactive streams, compile-time query verification, and instant startup times without cloud latency.
  • Zero Account Requirement: All student schedules, notes, and study timestamps remain on the user's local device filesystem.

Technical Stack
