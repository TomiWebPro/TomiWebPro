Problem & Motivation

During travels to China following graduation from high school, accessing international computational resources, academic databases, and personal home servers was severely impaired by state-level firewall filters and Deep Packet Inspection (DPI).

While tools like Tailscale and WireGuard provide incredible peer-to-peer connectivity under normal network conditions, their handshake signatures, UDP packet sizes, and unpadded crypto headers are trivial for modern DPI systems to detect and selectively drop or throttle.

Rather than relying on closed, commercial, or easily blockable consumer VPNs, I decided to engineer Stealthscale: combining the decentralized mesh topology of Tailscale/Headscale with custom transport obfuscation layers that masquerade as standard web traffic.

Protocol Architecture

stealthscale-traffic-flow.txt 
[CLIENT NODE A] (e.g., Laptop behind restrictive firewall)
       |
       |  1. Encapsulates WireGuard packet with randomized padding
       |  2. Wraps in standard TLS 1.3 ALPN h2 / WebSocket transport
       v
[INTERNET DPI GATEWAY] (Inspects packet: sees standard HTTPS to valid domain)
       |
       v
[STEALTH RELAY / PEER NODE B] (e.g., Home Proxmox Server)
       |
       |  3. Strips TLS envelope & decodes WireGuard payload
       |  4. Injects into virtual TUN interface
       v
[INTERNAL MESH NETWORK (100.64.0.0/10)]
Key Architectural Features

• Real TLS 1.3 Handshake Mimicry: Sessions negotiate valid cryptographic handshakes mimicking modern browser TLS fingerprints (JA3/JA4 and cipher suite ordering), eliminating synthetic handshake anomalies.

• Entropy & Padding Shaping: WireGuard packets typically have high uniform entropy and deterministic packet size increments. Stealthscale inserts variable padding and randomized timing jitter to match real web browsing traffic distributions.

• Self-Hosted Coordination Server: Engineered around Headscale primitives, ensuring zero reliance on third-party SaaS control planes.
