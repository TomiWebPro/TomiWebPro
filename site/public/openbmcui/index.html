Genesis at Computex & Compal Hardware Labs

In June 2026, Tomi Yang traveled to Taiwan to attend Computex 2026 and the NVIDIA Global Developer Conference (GDC), where he was invited to tour Compal's enterprise hardware testing laboratory.

While touring the testing floor, Yang observed engineers conducting validation tests on high-density GPU server clusters. Although physical steps like plugging high-speed InfiniBand cables naturally require manual labor, the software validation workflow was surprisingly repetitive: setting up network boots, reinstalling operating systems, flashing BIOS configurations, applying Mellanox/NVIDIA driver stacks, and executing bandwidth scripts.

Having recently built the computer-use and autonomous workflow harnesses in Cognithor, Yang realized these exact automation primitives could be ported directly into the Baseboard Management Controller (BMC) interface via the Redfish API.

Industry Impact & Open Source Roadmap

During the lab visit, Yang pitched this prototype directly to the Vice President of Compal. Recognizing its potential to drastically reduce human engineer overhead in validation labs, he offered Yang a paid internship on the spot.

Yang respectfully declined the offer to maintain full independent ownership and release OpenBMCUI as an open-source platform, building a foundation for universal, autonomous server management.
