Genesis at Computex & Compal Hardware Labs

In June 2026, I traveled to Taiwan to attend Computex 2026 and the NVIDIA Global Developer Conference (GDC), where I was invited to tour Compal's enterprise hardware testing laboratory.

While touring the testing floor, I observed engineers conducting validation tests on high-density GPU server clusters. Although physical steps like plugging high-speed InfiniBand cables naturally require manual labor, the software validation workflow was surprisingly repetitive: setting up network boots, reinstalling operating systems, flashing BIOS configurations, applying Mellanox/NVIDIA driver stacks, and executing bandwidth scripts.

Having recently built the computer-use and autonomous workflow harnesses in Cognithor, I realized these exact automation primitives could be ported directly into the Baseboard Management Controller (BMC) interface via the Redfish API.

Industry Impact & Independent Roadmap

During the lab visit, I pitched this prototype directly to the Vice President of Compal. Recognizing its potential to drastically reduce human engineer overhead in validation labs, he offered me a paid internship to develop the system within Compal.

I respectfully declined the offer to maintain full independent ownership and release OpenBMCUI as an open-source platform, building a foundation for universal, autonomous server management.
