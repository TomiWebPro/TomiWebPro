1. Overview & Philosophy

Zixuan "Tomi" Yang (born June 1, 2008, in Kunming, Yunnan, China), known online as TomiWebPro, is an 18-year-old developer and B.Sc. Information Engineering student at the Technical University of Munich (TUM) on the Heilbronn campus in Germany[1]. He is also Deputy Head of Technology at GoodIdeas, where he oversees web platform architecture alongside the CTO.

Tomi's engineering work centers on high-leverage software primitives: multi-autonomous agent orchestration harnesses, baseboard management controller (BMC) automation via DMTF Redfish for datacenter server racks, DPI-resistant mesh networking, and local-first, self-hosted software architectures. His design philosophy favors zero-dependency, local-first architectures, strict protocol transparency, and automated feedback loops over marketing abstractions.

2. Early Life & International Education

Born in Kunming, China, Tomi grew up troubleshooting household electronics for his grandparents and learning programming via Apple Swift Playgrounds on iPad. His mother worked for an international enterprise and later Bayer, which brought frequent international travel from an early age[2].

In 2016, at age 9, Tomi relocated with his mother to Budapest, Hungary, where the family obtained Hungarian permanent residency. He spent two years in a local Hungarian primary school — placed back from Grade 3 into Grade 2 to learn Hungarian — before transferring to The British International School Budapest (BISB), advancing directly from Grade 3 into Year 7.

Tomi's secondary education spanned four international and national schools across China and Hungary, developing multilingual fluency in English (IELTS 8.0), Mandarin, and German (ÖSD A2).

COVID-19 Return to China (2020–2023)

During the pandemic, Tomi returned to China: three months at the Fifth Middle School of Guandu District, Kunming (Oct 2021 – Jan 2022), followed by 1.5 years at Malvern College Chengdu (Jan 2022 – Jun 2023) on an Academic Merit Scholarship (100,000 CNY/year). When Computer Science classes were unavailable, he self-taught the entire IGCSE Computer Science syllabus through independent notes, and served as a student role model and stage presenter.

Return to BISB, Budapest (2023–2026)

Tomi returned to Budapest in 2023 following Chinese regulatory shifts around international A-Level schools. At BISB he completed 13 IGCSE subjects and acted as a cross-border hardware supplier from China for the school robotics club, sourcing microcontrollers and components for engineering projects.

3. Systems, AI & Engineering Work

Tomi has initiated, authored, or co-maintained 9 standalone open-source software projects across artificial intelligence, mesh networking, hardware interfaces, and utilities:

Cognithor (Multi-Autonomous Agent Harness)

In January 2026, inspired by early agent frameworks, Tomi co-developed Cognithor with a German collaborator (Alex8791-cyber →). Reaching over 100,000 lines of modular Python, the system incorporates long-term episodic memory, computer-use automation, and multi-agent coordination. Notably, the framework enabled open-weights Qwen 30B to solve approximately 30% of ARC-AGI-3 benchmark tasks, outperforming published Claude Opus 4.7 approaches[3]. The project earned 150+ organic GitHub stars.
Explore Cognithor Project Page →

Stealthscale (Undetectable Mesh Networking)

Developed in October 2026 after experiencing severe firewall restrictions during travel to China, Stealthscale builds upon Tailscale and Headscale primitives. It encapsulates WireGuard packets within authentic TLS 1.3/HTTP/2 frames, adding randomized padding and timing jitter to defeat deep packet inspection (DPI) and traffic analysis.
Explore Stealthscale Project Page →

OpenBMCUI (Redfish & BMC Automation)

In September 2026, following visits to Compal's server testing laboratory in Taiwan, Tomi created OpenBMCUI. The tool integrates DMTF Redfish REST APIs with an LLM orchestration engine to automate bare-metal server operating system installation, driver compilation, and InfiniBand bandwidth qualification.
Explore OpenBMCUI Project Page →

StudyKing (Adaptive Learning Assistant)

Launched in March 2026 during IB exam preparation as a replacement for platforms like Preply, StudyKing is an autonomous multimodal tutoring system developed continuously via 24/7 AI coding agents (initially DeepSeek) with daily commits. It ingests PDFs, lecture audio and video, slides, photos, and prompts, converting them into structured presentations, explanations, and progress-tracked quizzes for university STEM courses.
Explore StudyKing Project Page →

ECTS Tracker (Local-First Flutter Application)

Built in November 2026 in Budapest to pace remote TUM ECTS study-hour targets during the visa delay period, ECTS Tracker is a local-first mobile and desktop application written in Flutter (Dart) using Riverpod for reactive state management and Drift (SQLite) for zero-latency local persistence. It translates European Credit Transfer System (ECTS) credit quotas into daily and weekly study-hour pacing benchmarks, and has been shared with TUM classmates.
Explore ECTS Tracker Project Page →

PChess (Stockfish Engine & LLM Coach)

Developed as Tomi's IB Computer Science Internal Assessment (earning a Grade 7), PChess uses Stockfish to identify critical tactical swings in PGN game logs, then prompts an LLM coach to explain the conceptual positional motifs rather than merely displaying engine lines.
Explore PChess Project Page →

NIQE Modernized Fork

Authored in March 2025 for his IB Extended Essay, this project upgraded the 2014 MATLAB/Python Natural Image Quality Evaluator to modern Python 3.11+ and OpenCV, using Natural Scene Statistics (NSS) to evaluate visual fidelity in NVIDIA DLSS vs AMD FSR upscaling.
Explore NIQE Fork Project Page →

ActiveLearnBypass

Created in June 2024, this concurrent Python tool automated parallel retrieval of unauthenticated high-resolution textbook page images and losslessly compiled them into offline PDFs.
Explore ActiveLearnBypass Project Page →

PixelQuest (Bilingual 2D Pixel Platformer)

Built in November 2026, PixelQuest is a bilingual (English/Mandarin) 2D procedural pixel platformer in Java 17 and libGDX, assembled in a single overnight AI-assisted sprint as an architectural reference for a TUM Fundamentals of Programming team project. It was kept strictly separate from the group coursework submission to uphold academic integrity, with plans for a public App Store release.

Taiwan Compal Laboratory & OpenBMCUI Origin

While touring the testing floor, Tomi observed engineers conducting validation tests on high-density GPU server clusters. Although physical steps like plugging high-speed InfiniBand cables naturally require manual labor, the software validation workflow was surprisingly repetitive: setting up network boots, reinstalling operating systems, flashing BIOS configurations, applying Mellanox/NVIDIA driver stacks, and executing bandwidth scripts. The Vice President of Compal offered a paid internship on the spot; Tomi respectfully declined the offer to maintain full independent ownership and release the resulting system as open-source OpenBMCUI.
